> Method note: these notes come from web search result snippets and summaries only. Direct page fetches (WebFetch) failed with DNS errors for idealista.com, esdiario.com and forbes.es, and curl was blocked, so no figure was checked against the full original page. ## Key question 1: what do Idealista's sale price and gross rental yield reports say by capital (2024, 2025, 2026)?

### Takeaway
The national gross rental yield fell steadily: 7.2% (end 2024), 7.3% (Q1 2025), 7.2% (Q2 2025), 6.9% (Q3 2025), 6.7% (end 2025), 6.7% (Q1 2026), 6.5% (Q2 2026) and 6.2% (Q3 2026). The cause is that sale prices (+11.8% year on year in Q3 2026) are rising about twice as fast as asking rents (+5.6% year on year, September 2026). The highest-yield capitals are consistently mid-size or small cities: Murcia, Lleida, Jaén, Huelva, Castellón, Zamora and Segovia, at about 7-8%. San Sebastián (about 3.3-3.5%), Madrid and Barcelona are at the bottom.

### Inferences
- Nationally and in almost every capital, sale prices rose faster than rents in 2025-2026: prices +11.8% against rents +5.6% year on year in Q3 2026. That is why the national yield fell 0.7 points in a year, to 6.2%.
- Cities where the yield held up or rose (rent kept pace with prices): Huelva went from 7.1% (Q3 2025) to 7.4% (Q3 2026), and Castellón from 7.0% to 7.2%. They are the only clear cases in the snippets. These are inferred from the yield movement, not from separate rent and price series.
- Cities with the sharpest yield compression: Salamanca (5.7% to 4.9%, price +17.9%), Murcia (8.2% to below 8%), Barcelona and Lleida (rents falling) and Madrid (4.7% to about 4.3%). In Madrid prices rose only 2.4%, but rents rose 4.8%. That would normally raise the yield, so the disputed 4.3% figure for Madrid is suspect or reflects the change in the index methodology.
- For investment in student flats, mid-size cities with good yields (above 6.5%) are mostly in Andalucía, Murcia, Castellón, Extremadura and Lleida. The classic university cities (Salamanca, Granada, Valladolid, Oviedo) yield only about 4.5-5% on these headline measures, because sale prices are high relative to whole-flat rents. Room-by-room rental to students would raise the effective yield, but that is not captured by these indices.

- 2023 baseline figures by capital were not found.
- September 2026 sale €/m² is missing for most mid-size capitals.

## Key question 2: what do the Notariado, Registradores, INE IPV and Ministerio de Vivienda show?

### Takeaway
Only national and regional transaction-based figures were found. Notarial price in July 2026 was 2,112 €/m² (+7.7% year on year), with flats at 2,547 €/m² (+12.5%), while sales fell for a seventh consecutive month. Tinsa appraisal values rose 14.2-14.9% year on year in Q3 2026. No municipal tables from Registradores, the INE IPV or the Ministry of Housing were retrieved.

- Transaction prices (Notariado +7.7%) are rising more slowly than asking prices (idealista +11.8%) and appraisal values (Tinsa about +14-15%). Yields calculated on transaction prices would therefore be somewhat higher than Idealista's.

### Inferences
- Huelva rose from 7.1% to 7.4% and Castellón from 7.0% to 7.2% (Q3 2025 to Q3 2026), while Murcia and Lleida slipped. In Huelva and Castellón rents roughly kept up with or outpaced prices. In Murcia, Lleida, Salamanca and the big cities, prices outran rents.
- Zamora and Jaén held about 7-7.5%. Their low entry prices (Jaén 1,466 €/m²) keep the yield ratios high even as prices rise.

### Gaps
- No Banco de España (Informe de Estabilidad Financiera or Boletín Económico) city-level price-to-rent or overvaluation figures for 2025-2026 were found.
- No Tinsa or Fotocasa city-level price-to-rent ratio studies were found.
- For the 3-4 bedroom flat price (90-120 m²), only the derived estimates in the table above are available. No portal figures by flat size were found.
