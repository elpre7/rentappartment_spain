# Purpose-built student accommodation (residencias universitarias / PBSA) in Spain: supply, coverage and pipeline by city, 2023-2028

Method note: full-page fetching was blocked in this environment (egress proxy refused the publishers' domains), so every finding below comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages. The figures match across several outlets, but the original PDFs (Savills "Informe PBSA 2026", Atlas "Informe PBSA 2026", JLL, CBRE, Cushman & Wakefield) were not opened directly. Check the key figures against the originals before using them in an investment memo. Unless it says otherwise, "coverage" in a source means beds ÷ potential demand (displaced students), not beds ÷ all enrolled students. The two definitions give very different percentages (roughly 18% versus 7-8%).

## 1. Current bed counts and coverage ratios by city

### Takeaway
Spain had about 119,000-125,000 operating student beds at end-2025/2026, depending on the source. That covers only 1 in about 5-5.5 displaced students, and national provision is 7-8% of enrolled students against an EU average of 14-18%. Madrid (about 20,000 beds) has the largest stock in absolute terms. Valencia (8.6 displaced students per bed) is the most undersupplied large city. Salamanca, Girona, Murcia and A Coruña have the highest relative coverage.

### Inferences
- The figures of about 18% (beds ÷ displaced students) and 7-8% (beds ÷ all enrolled students) are both used in the press. Compare cities only within one source.
- Even Madrid, with the largest stock, covers only about 1 in 5 displaced students. Residences therefore act as a premium segment rather than a substitute for shared flats in every large city.

### Gaps
- No 2026 city-level bed counts were found for Barcelona (beyond the city council's 13,800 including planned beds), Sevilla, Málaga, Bilbao, Zaragoza or San Sebastián. Bilbao and the Basque Country are a clear gap.
- StuRents, Knight Frank, Colliers, Deloitte and BNP Paribas RE city tables were not found in search results.

## 2. Pipeline of new beds by city through 2028, and operators

### Takeaway
After roughly 9,000 beds opened in 2024, the pipeline is shrinking. Savills expects about 5,550 beds in 2026 (16 residences) and about 10,500 in 2027-2028. Madrid (5,144), Barcelona (2,635) and Valencia (2,388-3,069) lead. Sevilla (1,408) and Málaga (1,295) are next. Developers cite shortage of land and a shift from opportunistic capital to core funds that buy stabilised assets rather than build.

### Cited Findings
- Savills 2026: about 5,550 new beds expected in 2026 across 16 residences, plus about 10,500 in 2027-2028. - Atlas Informe PBSA 2026: **Valencia ranks 1st nationally with 12 projects and 3,069 beds (15.7% of Spain's pipeline)**, followed by **Sevilla 1,408 and Málaga 1,295**. ### Inferences
- Measured against demand, the pipeline is small. Madrid's 5,144 beds to 2028 are about 5% of its 107,000 demand. Valencia's 3,069 beds still leave about 5 students per bed. New PBSA is unlikely to cap shared-room rents at city level in any of the large cities before 2028. A local effect is possible near specific campuses where several projects open together (for example Valencia's Benimaclet/Tarongers/Burjassot area, or Zaragoza's Actur, where about 670 beds arrive in one go).
- Much of the "activity" is M&A (Resa, Greystar, the Livensa-Nido merger), not new beds.

### Gaps
- No 2026-2028 opening schedules were found for Xior, Micampus, The Social Hub, Vita Student or Amro outside Valencia. No Bilbao, San Sebastián or Santiago projects were found.
- Pipeline figures mix "under construction" and "planned" without a split. The breakdown is in the paid reports.

## 3. Undersupplied versus nearing-saturation cities

### Takeaway
All sources call Spain as a whole undersupplied. The most undersupplied large cities are Valencia (8.6 per bed), Barcelona (constrained by planning rules), Madrid and Sevilla/Granada/Málaga. No source in 2026 identifies a city as oversupplied. Salamanca, Girona, Murcia and A Coruña have the highest coverage, and Salamanca is described as a market with "real competition between operators" rather than excess supply.

### Cited Findings
- Valencia: 8.6 displaced students per bed versus 5.5 nationally. Valencia Plaza estimates the city would need about 10 times its current beds to cover demand — [Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/plaza-inmobiliaria/valencia-necesita-multiplicar-por-diez-sus-plazas-en-residencias-de-estudiantes-para-cubrir-la-demanda); [Valencia Plaza (Atlas 2026)](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/plaza-inmobiliaria/valencia-lidera-la-inversion-en-residencias-de-estudiantes-en-espana-con-12-proyectos-y-3069-camas)
- Salamanca: 30.7% coverage of displaced students (national 18.2%). The report adds that a low ratio does not mean excess supply but a market where competition between operators is already real — [La Gaceta de Salamanca 07/09/2026](https://www.lagacetadesalamanca.es/salamanca/salamanca-quinta-ciudad-pais-plazas-residencias-universitarias-20260907060000-nt.html)
- Barcelona: an amendment to the city's general land-use plan (the MPGM) limits new residences to a maximum of 350 places each and excludes them from the centre and the surrounding ring. A cap of about 3,500 places is also reported, and the reports don't reconcile the 350 and 3,500 figures. Price regulation for residences was initially approved at about €350-400 per room per month (Feb 2025) — [Eje Prime](https://www.ejeprime.com/mercado/barcelona-limita-la-capacidad-y-ubicacion-de-las-residencias-de-estudiantes); [Brainsre](https://brainsre.news/barcelona-limita-las-residencias-de-estudiantes-en-el-centro-de-la-ciudad/); [idealista 20/02/2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/02/20/833104-barcelona-regulara-los-precios-de-las-residencias-de-estudiantes-para-garantizar-su)
- Pamplona: most residences more than 92% occupied — [Noticias de Navarra](https://www.noticiasdenavarra.com/suplementos/2026/04/17/residencias-estudiantes-consolidan-solucion-alojamiento-10933325.html)
- Spain PBSA occupancy is about 97% (Bonard, 2023-24) — [Bonard](https://www.bonard.com/media/spain-in-the-global-top-10-in-pbsa-with-97-occupancy-and-rents-up-to-5)

### Inferences
- For a shared-flat investor, the most persistent shortage is in Valencia, Barcelona (where regulation restricts new PBSA in the centre), Madrid, Sevilla and Málaga. In Salamanca, PBSA competes most directly with shared flats, so room-rent upside there is more limited.

### Gaps
- No 2026 occupancy or rent-discount data by city that would signal saturation. No source names an oversupplied city.

## 4. Average monthly price of a residence bed by city and its change

### Takeaway
Residence beds cost roughly 1.5-2 times a room in a shared flat. Atlas gives a national average of €914/month and Madrid €1,251 (2024 data). Older JLL and Bonard figures give Madrid €780-804 and Barcelona €699-773. Shared rooms cost about €430 nationally and €590-735 in Madrid and Barcelona (2026). No authoritative 2026 city series for residence prices was found.

- Commercial blogs (low reliability) give €850-1,200 in Madrid and Barcelona, and €500-800 in mid-size cities — [Stephouse](https://stephouse.es/en/blog/cuanto-cuesta-residencia-de-estudiantes-espana/)

### Inferences
- Because residences sit well above shared rooms (which include services), PBSA supply mainly caps the top of the room market rather than the mid-market. In Barcelona, regulated residence pricing at about €350-400 would compete directly with shared rooms, but only for the limited new supply it applies to.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 residence price by city from Savills, JLL, CBRE or Atlas could be verified. The year-on-year change in residence prices by city is not available.

## 5. Public plans for student housing

### Takeaway
Public action is small relative to the deficit. The national programme funds about 1,525 affordable university units with €45M (first phase, announced Aug 2026), and Madrid's "Plan Vive Universitario" (public land, private build and operation) was announced in March 2026 without bed targets. Barcelona regulates residences more than it promotes them.

### Cited Findings
- The Ministry of Housing (MIVAU) gives €45M to 7 entities for a first phase of 1,525 affordable units for students and university staff. The 7 projects are in 6 regions: Castilla-La Mancha, Andalucía, Madrid, Cataluña, Castilla y León and Comunidad Valenciana. Maximum prices will include utilities and wifi. The programme comes from an October 2024 inter-ministerial protocol and fits within the Plan Estatal de Vivienda 2026-2030. No opening dates have been given. CRUE (the Spanish universities' association) says public places remain insufficient — [MIVAU 31/08/2026](https://www.mivau.gob.es/el-ministerio/sala-de-prensa/noticias/lun-31082026-1741); [idealista 01/09/2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/activos-alternativos/2026/09/01/911966-el-gobierno-impulsa-con-45-millones-el-alojamiento-asequible-de); [que.es](https://www.que.es/2026/09/02/alojamientos-universitarios-asequibles); [Brainsre](https://brainsre.news/vivienda-1525-alojamientos-universitarios-asequibles/)
- Plan Estatal de Vivienda 2026-2030: €7,000M in total, including youth rent aid of up to €300/month that regions can adjust — [MIVAU](https://www.mivau.gob.es/vivienda/info-plan-estatal-de-vivienda-2026-2030); [idealista](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/activos-alternativos/2026/09/01/911966-el-gobierno-impulsa-con-45-millones-el-alojamiento-asequible-de)
- Comunidad de Madrid, "Plan Vive Universitario" (announced March 2026): the region provides public land and the private sector builds and operates, with regional supervision of quality and prices. Talks are under way with municipalities that host or border campuses. No bed numbers or timetable have been given — [Madrid Actual](https://www.madridactual.es/noticias-regionales/economia-y-empleo/vivienda/madrid-ayuso-lanza-plan-vive-universitario-construir-20260305-8089165.html); [Noticias Paracuellos](https://noticiasparacuellosdejarama.es/la-comunidad-de-madrid-lanza-el-plan-vive-universitario-nuevas-residencias-para-atraer-talento-y-bajar-el-precio-del-alquiler-en-madrid/)
- Barcelona: capacity and location limits plus price regulation for residences (see section 3) — [Eje Prime](https://www.ejeprime.com/mercado/barcelona-limita-la-capacidad-y-ubicacion-de-las-residencias-de-estudiantes); [idealista](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/02/20/833104-barcelona-regulara-los-precios-de-las-residencias-de-estudiantes-para-garantizar-su)
- Málaga: the city council is tendering municipal land for student residences (Jan 2025) — [El Español Málaga](https://www.elespanol.com/malaga/malaga-ciudad/20250104/malaga-sacara-nuevo-mercado-suelos-construccion-residencias-estudiantes-mayores/911658840_0.html)
- UPV/EHU: a tender for a residence on the Gipuzkoa campus (details not verified) — [TED](https://ted.europa.eu/es/notice/99536-2026/pdf)

### Inferences
- The 1,525 public units are equivalent to about 0.3% of the 528,000-bed deficit. Public supply will not cap private room rents in any city in 2026-2028.

### Gaps
- No Generalitat de Catalunya or Generalitat Valenciana student-housing programme with figures was found. The list of the 7 funded universities and their bed counts was not obtained.
