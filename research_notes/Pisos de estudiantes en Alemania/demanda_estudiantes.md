# Student housing demand drivers in German university cities, 2022-2028

Research date: 18 October 2026. Method note: direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy in this session (studierendenwerke.de, studis-online.de and others refused), so every figure below comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the full documents. City-level tables (Destatis by Hochschulort, DSW Unterbringungsquote per city, Wissenschaft weltoffen per university) could therefore only be filled in part. Each such shortfall is listed under "Gaps". Status labels: **[confirmed]** = official/final data, **[provisional]** = preliminary official data or university press release, **[announced]** = plan or forecast, **[estimate]** = third-party estimate.

## 1. Student numbers by university city and trend (WS 2023/24 to 2026/27)

### Takeaway
National student numbers are roughly flat at about 2.87-2.88 million (WS 2025/26, +0.4% on the year). Demand is moving between regions. Bavaria (G9 switch in 2025) and Hessen are shrinking, and NRW and Schleswig-Holstein lose an Abitur cohort in 2026. East German states and the city-states are forecast to grow modestly to 2035, while western Flächenländer shrink. The official forecast puts the low point of first-year students in 2025-2026, with a recovery from 2027.

### Cited Findings
**National**
- WS 2025/26: student numbers rose 0.4% on the year to about 2.88 million (Destatis press release PD25_426_21, Nov 2025) **[provisional]**. Universities lost slightly while Fachhochschulen gained — [Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2025/11/PD25_426_21.html); [Studis Online](https://www.studis-online.de/Studieren/art-3099-studentenzahlen-2025-2026.php)
- About 492,000 first-year students in study year 2025, above earlier forecasts. Destatis gives +0.3% for first-years in 2025 nationally and −13% in Bavaria — [fernstudi.net citing Destatis](https://www.fernstudi.net/news/hochschulen-verzeichnen-mehr-studierende-als-erwartet); [Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2025/11/PD25_426_21.html)
- Haufe calls 2.88 million a "new record". This is doubtful: the all-time peak was about 2.94 million in WS 2021/22 (from background knowledge, not verified in this session). Treat "record" claims with caution — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/immobilien/investment/investment-marktpotenzial-von-studentenwohnungen_256_492340.html)
- **Forecast (KMK/Wissenschaftsministerkonferenz, decided 17 Sep 2025, period 2024-2035)** **[announced/forecast]**:
  - First-year students fall by more than 6% to about 380,300 in the current and next year, because G9 returns remove a regular Abitur cohort: Bavaria 2025, NRW and Schleswig-Holstein 2026, Saarland 2029.
  - First-year numbers rise again from 2027.
  - The total number of students only recovers around 2030, rising from about 2.26 million then to 2.37 million in 2035.
  - Sources: [KMK text 2025-09-17](https://www.kmk.org/fileadmin/Dateien/pdf/Statistik/Dokumentationen/2025-09-17_Text_VB_Studienanfaenger-Studierende_neueHP.pdf); [KMK press](https://www.kmk.org/aktuelles/pressearchiv/mitteilung/kurzfristiger-rueckgang-langfristiger-aufschwung-studienanfaengerzahlen-sollen-ab-2027-deutlich-steigen.html)
  - Note: the 2.26 million base appears to use a different counting basis than Destatis' 2.88 million (the KMK model probably excludes some groups, such as distance learners or certain private institutions). Not reconciled.
- **Regional forecast (KMK, reported by Forschung & Lehre)** **[forecast]**: total students change versus 2023 by about −6% in western Flächenländer, +3% in eastern Flächenländer and +7.7% in the city-states (Berlin, Hamburg, Bremen) — [Forschung & Lehre](https://www.forschung-und-lehre.de/lehre/studienanfaengerzahlen-steigen-wieder-ab-2027-7295)

### Inferences
- **Short-term dip (WS 2025/26-2026/27) in Bavaria, NRW and Schleswig-Holstein first-year cohorts.** Cities hit:
  - Bavaria: München, Würzburg, Regensburg, Erlangen/Nürnberg, Augsburg, Bamberg, Passau.
  - NRW: Köln, Bonn, Münster, Aachen, Bochum, Dortmund, Duisburg-Essen, Bielefeld, Paderborn, Siegen.
  - Schleswig-Holstein: Kiel, Lübeck.
  - Each faces a missing cohort that passes through about 3-5 years of enrolment, which slightly loosens WG demand around 2026-2029. This is a timing effect, not a structural one. KMK expects first-years to rise again from 2027. Big, supply-tight cities (München, Köln, Münster) will probably stay undersupplied anyway.
- **Structural direction to 2035 favours city-states and the east:** Berlin, Hamburg, Bremen (+7.7%) and Leipzig, Dresden, Jena, Magdeburg (eastern states +3%), against −6% for western Flächenländer. This is a forecast and rests on unchanged study propensity and migration assumptions.
- **Growth in the east is driven by international students, not domestic ones.** Sachsen-Anhalt was flat overall but had +9.4% foreign students. Jena reported record international intake. International students depend more on WG and furnished rooms (see section 2).
- **Hessen is shrinking** (−1.5%; Frankfurt, Darmstadt, Gießen, Marburg, Kassel). This is a mild negative signal for small Hessian university towns.

## 2. International student inflows (DAAD / DZHW Wissenschaft weltoffen 2024-2026)

### Takeaway
International enrolment is the main growth driver: it rose from about 402,000 (WS 2024/25) to about 420,000-426,000 (WS 2025/26). International first-years reached a record of about 130,000, mostly in Master's programmes, and India is the largest source country. Figures by university were not retrievable in this session.

### Cited Findings
- WS 2024/25: 402,083 international students (+6% on the year), about two-thirds at universities and one-third at Fachhochschulen. India is the top origin country with about 49,000 (in some extracts), and 116,600 international first-years was a record at the time **[confirmed]** — [DAAD PM 2025](https://www.daad.de/de/pressemitteilungen/2025/erneut-hohe-zahl-internationaler-studierender-in-deutschland/); [DAAD PM](https://www.daad.de/de/pressemitteilungen/2025/zahl-internationaler-studierender-deutlich-ueber-400000/)
- WS 2025/26, DAAD quick survey of 212 universities: about 420,000 international students and doctoral candidates, and +9% international first-years, rising especially in Master's programmes **[provisional]** — [idw / DAAD Schnellabfrage](https://idw-online.de/de/news863772); [DAAD EN](https://www.daad.de/en/press-releases/zahl-internationaler-studierender-deutlich-ueber-400000/)
- Wissenschaft weltoffen 2026: about 426,200 international students in WS 2025/26 and about 130,000 international first-years (new high). India is the main origin country with about 69,000. The year of the India figure is unclear in the extract. The previous year India had about 59,000 (+20%) **[confirmed, official]** — [verbaende.com WWO 2026 press](http://www.verbaende.com/news/pressemitteilung/wissenschaft-weltoffen-2026-deutschland-bleibt-weltweit-gefragter-standort-fuer-studium-und-promotion-175029/); [Forschung & Lehre](https://www.forschung-und-lehre.de/karriere/mehr-internationales-wissenschaftspersonal-als-je-zuvor-7952)
- States, WS 2023/24: NRW 78,500, Bayern 61,400, Berlin 40,800 international students — [DAAD PM 2025](https://www.daad.de/de/pressemitteilungen/2025/zahl-internationaler-studierender-deutlich-ueber-400000/)
- Sachsen-Anhalt WS 2025/26: foreign students +9.4% (Magdeburg/OVGU is the main driver) — [StaLa Sachsen-Anhalt](https://statistik.sachsen-anhalt.de/daten-und-veroeffentlichungen/pressemitteilungen/2026/07/186/2026-94-mehr-auslaendische-studierende-im-wintersemester-2025/26-an-den-hochschulen-in-sachsen-anhalt)
- About 2,400 English-taught degree programmes (summer 2025) — [DAAD 2026 PM](https://www.daad.de/de/pressemitteilungen/2026/akademischer-austausch-zwischen-globalen-krisen-und-wachsender-bedeutung/)
- TUM Campus Heilbronn: 1,007 students in WS 2024/25, with an 83% international share (third-party guide) **[estimate/unverified]** — [mygermanuniversity](https://www.mygermanuniversity.com/de/universities/TUM-Campus-Heilbronn)
- A commercial site (y-axis) claims 69,816 Indian students (+17.5%) and a total of 516,820 for 2025/26. The total conflicts with DAAD, so it is unreliable — [y-axis](https://de.y-axis.com/news/germany-17-5-percent-rise-in-indian-students/)

### Inferences
- About 130,000 international first-years per year, at roughly 15% of all first-years, is the fastest-growing slice of WG and furnished-room demand. The international segment rose about 6% per year while domestic numbers were flat or falling.
- International Master's students concentrate where there are English-taught STEM programmes: TU/RWTH/TUM cities, Magdeburg, Chemnitz, Ilmenau, Jena, Darmstadt, Kaiserslautern and Siegen are typical (based on known programme patterns, not verified per university here). In eastern cities this partly offsets domestic demographic decline. Examples: OVGU +4.1% with state foreign +9.4%; Jena's record international intake.
- Risk factors to monitor: visa and political changes, and the "abroad" budget. The DAAD 2026 press release headline mentions "global crises". The Indian inflow is concentrated, so one source country accounts for about 16% of all international students.

### Gaps
- Wissenschaft weltoffen per-university rankings (for example TUM, RWTH, TU Berlin, KIT, TU Dresden, TU Chemnitz, OVGU) could not be retrieved. Check the WWO 2025/2026 open-access PDF (OAPEN / wbv) or the Destatis table on foreign students by university.
- No per-city international inflow trend for 2023 to 2026 is available.

## 3. New universities, campuses, faculties and medical expansions (2023-2028)

### Takeaway
Medical-school expansion is the most concrete capacity driver: Augsburg, the Bielefeld ramp-up, Chemnitz MEDiC, Bayreuth and Cottbus/Lausitz. Each adds only about 50-300 places per cohort, but these are long-duration (6-year) students with strong demand for rooms. TUM Heilbronn is a small, highly international niche.

### Cited Findings
- National: about 10,683 human-medicine places at 42 programmes for WS 2026/27, against about 10,000-10,300 for 2025/26 (Ratgeber site) **[estimate]** — [praktischarzt](https://www.praktischarzt.de/medizinstudium/medizinstudium-wintersemester/); [medizinerschmiede](https://www.medizinerschmiede.de/ratgeber/medizinstudienplaetze-bundeslaender-2026)
- Augsburg: 289 places for WS 2026/27, up from 168 in 2025/26, a jump of 121, said to be "almost all" of Bavaria's increase of 123 places (Ratgeber) **[estimate; cross-check with Uni Augsburg]** — [ncrechner](https://www.ncrechner.de/ratgeber/zulassungsrechner/nc-medizin-bayern-auswahlgrenzen-studienplaetze/); [bildungsklick: 2,264 medicine first-year places in Bavaria](https://bildungsklick.de/hochschule-und-forschung/detail/studienplaetze-medizin-erstsemester-bayern)
- Bavaria plans 2,700 additional medicine places by 2032/33 **[announced]** — [bildungsklick](https://bildungsklick.de/hochschule-und-forschung/detail/studienplaetze-medizin-erstsemester-bayern)
- Bielefeld (OWL medical faculty): 120 places for WS 2025/26 according to one listing, 60 in older listings (ramp-up) **[estimate, inconsistent]** — [medgurus](https://medgurus.de/blog/medizin/bewerbung/deutschland/studienorte/)
- Chemnitz MEDiC (TU Dresden with Klinikum Chemnitz): 50 places per winter semester — [mission-medizin](https://mission-medizin.de/kurs/medizin-studieren/lektionen/wenn-es-nicht-sofort-klappt/)
- Erlangen-Nürnberg/Bayreuth (Medizincampus Oberfranken): 55 places in both winter and summer semesters — [studimed](https://www.studimed.de/wo-medizin-im-ss-vs-ws-studieren/)
- Medizinische Universität Lausitz – Carl Thiem (Cottbus): teaching starts in WS 2026/27 according to one Ratgeber source **[announced; start not independently confirmed]** — [praktischarzt](https://www.praktischarzt.de/medizinstudium/medizinstudium-wintersemester/)
- TUM Campus Heilbronn: opened 2018, 1,007 students in WS 2024/25 (83% international) **[estimate]** — [mygermanuniversity](https://www.mygermanuniversity.com/de/universities/TUM-Campus-Heilbronn)

### Inferences
- Medical expansions matter at the margin for smaller cities. In Augsburg, about 289 new medical students per year times 6 years gives about 1,700 students at steady state (late 2020s to early 2030s). In Bielefeld, 120 per year gives about 700; in Cottbus, ramp-up from 2026. Medical students stay longer and are more likely to rent stable WG rooms.
- In large cities (München, Berlin) such expansions are immaterial relative to the base.

### Gaps
- No verified data on Landarztquote volumes, on new Hochschulen founded 2023-2028, on TUM Heilbronn targets after 2025, or on major non-medical programme expansions per city.
- Lausitz first-cohort size was not retrieved (planned about 200 per year from background knowledge; unverified).

## 4. Dormitory supply (Studierendenwerk), waiting lists, Junges Wohnen, private PBSA pipeline

### Takeaway
Public dorms house only about 10% of students. The national Unterbringungsquote is 10.12% (DSW 2025), down from 11.86% in 2007. Waiting lists at semester start are concentrated in about 11 high-pressure Studierendenwerke, with 33,005 students waiting on 30 Sep 2025, including more than 8,800 in München. Junges Wohnen funded 8,864 places in 2024, which is small relative to the gap. Private Mikroapartment/PBSA pipelines cover only about 18% of the identified shortfall, and that is mostly in the top-7 cities.

### Cited Findings
**Dorm supply**
- DSW "Wohnraum für Studierende – Statistische Übersicht 2025" (reference date 1 Jan 2025): national Unterbringungsquote 10.12%, versus 11.86% in 2007 **[confirmed]** — [DSW PDF 2025](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/fileadmin/user_upload/DSW_Wohnenraumstatisik_25.pdf)
- DSW 2024 overview, München row: 11,259 places, 112,450 students, 10.01% — [DSW 2024 PDF](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/fileadmin/user_upload/241212_DSW_Wohnen24_web.pdf)
- Members of the Studierendenwerke run about 1,700 residences with about 196,000 places. All publicly supported student housing places in 2024 totalled 240,728 (DSW, cited by the government) — [DSW](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/mangelware-wohnraum-33000-studierende-auf-wohnheim-wartelisten); [Bundestag hib 459/2025](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/kurzmeldungen-1112536)
- Savills (Jun 2025): only 8.3% of students were housed in dorms in WS 2024/25. Vacancy in the 30 largest university cities was 1.1%. Asking rents rose about 52% in 10 years, and about 58% for one-room flats. Furnished all-in micro-apartments mostly start at €600 per month — [Savills PDF](https://pdf.euro.savills.co.uk/germany-research/2025/studentisches-wohnen-in-deutschland.pdf); [immobilienmanager](https://www.immobilienmanager.de/studentisches-wohnen-bietet-neues-entwicklungspotenzial-26062025)
- City quotas from the Leibniz Nationalatlas (DSW data around 2023): Heidelberg 22.3% (about 15% without 2,310 private or church publicly supported places) versus Leipzig 12.8%. Berlin, München, Hamburg and Köln have relatively low quotas — [Nationalatlas](https://nationalatlas.de/nadbeitrag/wohnversorgung-studierender-grosse-diskrepanzen-zwischen-den-hochschulstaedten/); [Nationalatlas PDF](https://aktuell.nationalatlas.de/wp-content/uploads/24_04_studentisches_Wohnen.pdf)

**Waiting lists**
- 30 Sep 2025, DSW: 33,005 students were on waiting lists at 11 Studierendenwerke: Berlin, Darmstadt, Erlangen-Nürnberg, Frankfurt, Göttingen, Hamburg, Hannover, Heidelberg, Köln, Mainz and München. More than 8,800 of them were at Studierendenwerk München Oberbayern **[confirmed]** — [DSW press](https://www.studierendenwerke.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/mangelware-wohnraum-33000-studierende-auf-wohnheim-wartelisten); [Studierendenwerk Aachen repost](https://www.studierendenwerk-aachen.de/de/aktuelles/beitrag/dsw-mangelware-wohnraum.html)
- Berlin, around semester start WS 2026/27 (dpa): about 3,300 applicants on the waiting list, with 9,122 dorm places. More than 200,000 students against just under 10,000 Studierendenwerk places. Airbnb and TU Berlin funded temporary housing for 250 first-years — [Tagesspiegel/dpa](https://www.tagesspiegel.de/berlin/dpa-zu-wenige-wohnheimplatze-vor-neuem-uni-semester-in-berlin-16089614.html); [Berliner Kurier](https://www.berliner-kurier.de/article/studenten-berlin-wohnungsnot-semesterstart-10485352)
- Baden-Württemberg ministry: waiting lists overstate demand because of multiple applications — [Stuttgarter Zeitung](https://www.stuttgarter-zeitung.de/lokales/baden-wuerttemberg/mangel-in-baden-wuerttemberg-studentenbude-gesucht-die-dauernde-not-zum-semesterstart-79137971.html); [Landtag BW Drs. 17/9527](https://www.landtag-bw.de/resource/blob/597636/1370abbf285664bc96536c81f4399914/17_9527_D.pdf)
- Dresden: waits of several months, especially for single apartments at winter semester — [Studentenwerk Dresden FAQ](https://www.studentenwerk-dresden.de/wohnen/faq-40.html)

**Junges Wohnen (federal-state programme)**
- 2024: 8,864 dorm places for students and trainees funded: Bayern 2,371, Baden-Württemberg 1,319, NRW 1,152. Federal plus state subsidies totalled €628.6 million **[confirmed]** — [Bundestag hib 459/2025](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/hib/kurzmeldungen-1112536); [BT-Drs. 21/1790](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/017/2101790.pdf)

**Private PBSA / Mikroapartments**
- Micro-Living-Atlas 2026 (Belform, Oct 2026): a gap of about 73,500 micro-apartments. The current pipeline closes only about 18% of it. ### Inferences
- Shortage is structural in the 11 waiting-list Studierendenwerk regions: München, Berlin, Hamburg, Köln, Frankfurt, Darmstadt, Heidelberg, Mainz, Göttingen, Hannover and Erlangen-Nürnberg. Public new supply (about 9,000 Junges Wohnen places per year nationally) plus private PBSA (18% of the gap) will not close it by 2028. These are the cities where a 3-4 room WG flat is least exposed to vacancy risk, even with the G9 dip in München and Erlangen.
- Göttingen, Darmstadt and Mainz stand out as mid-size cities with formal waiting-list pressure. Their rent and purchase-price levels are typically below München and Frankfurt.
- PBSA pipelines concentrate in the top-7 cities. Mid-size cities therefore get little new private competition for furnished rooms, which helps WG investors there.
- Leipzig combines strong rent growth (more than 6% per year) with a low dorm quota (12.8%) and a forecast of east-German growth. Chemnitz has the lowest rents (€296), which means cheap entry but weak pricing power. Its upside depends on international and MEDiC demand.
- Regulatory risk: legislative tightening on furnished or short-term rentals (bulwiengesa warning) could affect furnished WG-room pricing. A German tax-resident investor should check the current Mietpreisbremse rules for furnished rentals.
