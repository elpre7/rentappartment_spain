Research date: 18 Oct 2026. **Method note:** the sandbox's network policy blocked direct page fetching for almost every host (baufi24, immowelt, greix.de, empirica, postbank, interhyp, drklein, mcmakler and others returned 403). ## 1. Price levels and YoY change by city, 2024-2026

### Takeaway
After the 2022-2023 correction, German condo prices recovered from mid-2024 and have been almost flat since late 2025. GREIX (transaction data) shows ETW +0.4% YoY nominal in Q2 2026, which is −2.1% real. Asking prices (immowelt) rose +0.7% QoQ in Q3 2026 to 3,288 €/m² nationally. The cities split: Düsseldorf, Essen, Stuttgart and Bonn are rising; Dortmund, Duisburg, Hannover and Nürnberg are slipping; Leipzig and Köln are flat.

## 2. Gross yields / Kaufpreisfaktoren by city; which university cities offer >4.5-5%?

### Takeaway
Gross yields have been edging up since 2022 because rents are rising faster than prices. Baufi24 puts the 52-city average at 4.17% in H1 2026 (H2 2025: 4.1%; about +0.12 pp per year). Postbank's average price-to-rent multiplier fell from 24.8 (2024) to 24.4 (2025). Above 5% are mainly Chemnitz (5.77%) and Ruhr cities: Gelsenkirchen 5.16%, Hagen 5.13% and Duisburg 5.05% (H2 2025). Among the target university cities, Chemnitz is above 5%. Magdeburg, Halle, Leipzig, Duisburg, Dortmund and possibly Bremen and Essen sit around 4.3-5% (own calc./estimates). The big metros and the southern university cities are at 3-3.5%.

### Cited Findings
- Baufi24 Mietrenditeatlas H1 2026 (18.08.2026), 52 cities: average gross yield 4.17% (H2 2025: 4.1%). Chemnitz is first at 5.77% (H2 2025: 5.90%), then Gelsenkirchen at 5.16%. Hamburg is lowest at 3.0%, and Stuttgart is the best of the Top-7 at 3.99%. All 52 cities are at least 3.00%. The average purchase price is 3,587 €/m². Purchase prices "nearly stagnate", and Leipzig, Hamburg, Köln and Stuttgart are named among the few big cities with rising prices. Method: purchase prices of existing flats vs asking rents on new lettings. — [Baufi24 press](https://www.baufi24.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/mietrendite-trend-082026/); [finanznachrichten/EQS](https://www.finanznachrichten.de/nachrichten-2026-08/69337781-eqs-media-baufi24-mietrenditeatlas-bruttomietrenditen-steigen-auf-4-17-prozent-kaufpreise-treten-nahezu-auf-der-stelle-022.htm); [VUB-Makler briefing](https://www.vub-makler.de/investoren-briefing-%C2%B7-ausgabe-11/)

- **Rents growing faster than prices (yields improving):** this holds nationally (GREIX rents +3.0-3.7% YoY vs ETW prices +0.4% YoY). The clearest city cases are **Köln** (rents +6.0% YoY, prices ~flat: +0.1% QoQ, 0.0% QoQ asking), **Leipzig** (rents +2.8% YoY, prices −0.1% QoQ; but Leipzig's 2026 rent growth is the weakest of the 15 big cities), **Dresden and Bremen** (rents +3.4% YTD vs prices +0.4%/+1.4% QoQ), **Hannover and Nürnberg** (prices falling, Nürnberg rents +3.0%), and **Dortmund** (prices −1.0% QoQ in Q3). - **University cities likely ≥4.5% gross:** Chemnitz (≈5.8%, confirmed). Likely ≈4.5-5.5% (not confirmed per city): Magdeburg, Halle, Leipzig, Duisburg (5.05% H2 2025), Dortmund, Bremen and probably Essen, Bochum, Kassel and Gießen. Below 4% (factor >25): München, Hamburg, Frankfurt, Berlin, Köln, Stuttgart, Freiburg, Heidelberg, Regensburg, Erlangen, Tübingen, Konstanz, Münster, Mainz and Darmstadt.

## 3. Mortgage rates (10-year fixed) 2025-2026 vs yields

### Takeaway
10-year fixed Bauzinsen rose from about 3.6% (Oct 2025) to about 4.4-4.5% (mid-Oct 2026), driven by the Iran conflict and energy price shock and rising Bund and Pfandbrief yields. With most university-city gross yields at 3-5%, buying with leverage now shows **negative leverage** almost everywhere. Only Chemnitz-type markets (≈5.8% gross) exceed the borrowing cost before costs.

### Cited Findings
- Interhyp: 10-year fixed 4.42% on 15.10.2026; another Interhyp page gives 4.5% for the same date. A year earlier it was about 3.6%. 10-year Pfandbrief yield 3.96%. Bank panel: 80% expect stable rates over 1-2 months; medium term, 60% expect falling and 40% rising. — [Interhyp Zinsen](https://www.interhyp.de/zinsen/); [cash-online](https://www.cash-online.de/a/interhyp-bauzinsen-bleiben-auf-hohem-niveau-stabil-727685/)
- Dr. Klein (as of 09.10.2026): top effective rates 4.16% to 4.71% depending on fixed period, with the 4.16% example based on a 10-year term. — [Dr. Klein](https://www.drklein.de/finanzlexikon/darlehenszinsen.html)
- Schwäbisch Hall (27.09.2026): 10-year rates "again above 4.0%". — [Schwäbisch Hall](https://www.schwaebisch-hall.de/ratgeber/immobilie-kaufen/zinsentwicklung.html)
- Baufi24 (June 2026): 10-year rates in a 3-4% corridor at that time. — [Baufi24](https://www.baufi24.de/bauzinsen/)

### Inferences
- The spread between gross yield and the 10-year rate is now about −1 pp for the average city (4.17% vs ~4.4%) and −1.4 pp for Hamburg. It is about +1.3 pp only for Chemnitz. Net yields after non-recoverable costs, maintenance, vacancy and purchase costs (Grunderwerbsteuer 3.5-6.5% plus notary and broker) are typically 0.8-1.5 pp lower than gross (rule-of-thumb estimate, not sourced). On that basis, only the highest-yield East German and Ruhr markets approach break-even against financing costs.
- Higher rates since summer 2026 explain the renewed weakness in GREIX liquidity indicators and the slowdown in prices.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain Bundesbank MFI statistics on average effective rates for 2025-2026.
